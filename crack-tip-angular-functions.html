Angular functions fij(θ)

Reference tables for the leading singular term of the crack-tip field, σij = K / √(2πr) · fij(θ), for each loading mode, in Cartesian and polar components, with the matching displacements.

What changes with geometry, and what does not

fij(θ) depends on the loading mode only

The angular functions are the same for every geometry: a centre crack, an edge crack, a compact tension specimen or a crack in a pressure vessel all share them. They change only with the mode of loading, that is, how the crack faces move relative to each other. Geometry and load magnitude enter in two places:

  1. the stress intensity factors KI, KII, KIII, through Y(a/W), and
  2. the higher-order terms of the Williams series [4], starting with the T-stress, which control how far from the tip the singular term stays accurate.

Combined loading

Mixed mode: add the fields, never the K's

In linear elasticity the three fields superpose, each with its own K:

(1)σij = [ KI fijI(θ) + KII fijII(θ) + KIII fijIII(θ) ] / √(2πr) KI + KII is meaningless because the angular functions differ. Energy does add: G = (KI² + KII²)/E′ + KIII²/2μ. Source: Irwin [5]; Anderson [9] · Notes §8.2

Beyond the singular term

The T-stress is where geometry shows up in the field

The full Williams expansion [4] for mode I is

(2)σij = KI/√(2πr) · fijI(θ) + T δ1iδ1j + O(√r) T is a constant stress parallel to the crack (it adds only to σxx). It depends on geometry and loading, and changes constraint and the size of the zone where K alone describes the field. Source: Williams [4]; Leevers & Radon [11] · Notes §8.3

For finite specimens T is usually given through the biaxiality ratio B = T√(πa)/KI, tabulated by Leevers and Radon (1982) [11] and Sherry et al. (1995) [12].
